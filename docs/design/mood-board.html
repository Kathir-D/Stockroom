BHow the asset list is shaped

You have photos per asset, but also 200+ near-identical units (ten T7i bodies). Scanning a list of names and recognising a picture are different jobs.

B3 + B1Settled. B3 is the base and every model row opens like an accordion onto its B1 unit table. One shape does both jobs: a student who wants "a T7i battery" reads the count and presses Add, and anyone who needs a specific body opens the row and picks the serial.

Collapses your 3rd category level into counts. A student wants "a T7i battery", not T7iBat-007 specifically, so the system assigns a free unit. Massively reduces list length; expand a row to see individual serials.

What the merge costs in backend work: the model row needs a per-model available count, which ListAssets does not return today, and pressing Add on a model row has to resolve to some free unit. EWhere the cart lives

The cart is frontend-only state until checkout. It has to be visible enough that nobody walks off with an uncommitted cart.

E1 → pageSettled. The cart lives on the bottom edge exactly like E1, but its button navigates to a full cart page instead of expanding a drawer. That is the shape every student already knows from shopping online, and it gives the due-date picker and the admin custodian picker room to breathe without eating the browse width.

SerialC70200-001 CustodianAlex Rivera · 200481 Checked out2026-09-05 14:32 Due2026-09-12

Anything printed on a barcode sticker renders in mono. Makes serials and student numbers unmistakably machine data, and 0/O and 1/l stop being ambiguous when someone reads one aloud. Costs a second font file.

Deliberately absent from every option: purple/indigo gradients, glassmorphism, glow effects, neon accents on dark, oversized border-radius, animated blobs, and colour used decoratively. Hue in this app means state and nothing else. That constraint is what makes it read as a tool rather than a template.
